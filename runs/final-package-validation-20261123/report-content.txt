Genetic Algorithms for<br/>Adversarial Testing

Implementation, development evidence and final evaluation<br/>23 November 2026

Executive summary

The project implements gradient-free genetic searches for a fixed pretrained VGG-11, using different perturbation representations for L∞, L2 and L0 and an optional Perlin-fog genotype. A protected successful archive separates search fitness from the final objective. The submitted bundle contains 15 required adversarial tensors and five bonus fog cases, their original classification visualizations, legal fog expressions and the two required implementations.

The strongest verified source was retained after two later global/local scheduling designs failed selection. A final, frozen Imagenette assessment succeeded on all 10 inputs for each of four attacks (40/40), including independent one-thread verification. This supports input transfer for the tested configuration; it neither establishes universal generalization nor proves that the perturbations are globally minimal.

1. Task, baseline and scoring contract

The supplied ZIP and README define the interfaces and scoring [1]. Both original attack modules contain unimplemented entry points. Two independent clean CPU runs reproduced all five expected classes; that established the reference predictions, not an adversarial optimization baseline. The final code preserves the instructor utilities, grader and the three supplied fog-rendering functions.

Let x be the normalized 1 × 3 × 224 × 224 tensor returned by the unchanged loader. That loader resizes the image and applies the VGG weight transforms; distances are not RGB 8-bit pixel distances. The attack infers y = argmax p(x), rather than looking up a filename or label. Candidate images are clipped to [−3, 3], matching the model wrapper. A successful return must change the actual argmax.

D∞(δ) = max<sub>i</sub> |δ<sub>i</sub>| &nbsp;&nbsp; D2(δ) = Σ<sub>i</sub> δ<sub>i</sub>² &nbsp;&nbsp; D0(δ) = Σ<sub>i</sub> 1[δ<sub>i</sub> ≠ 0]

<b>Two important grading details:</b> the supplied “L2” helper reports the sum of squares, not its square root; L0 counts changed channel elements, not RGB pixels. The five seed reference IDs are knot 616, mink 357, orca 148, sock 806 and tank 847.

Adversarial-example research motivates small, deliberate perturbations [2,3]; sparse evolutionary attacks motivate support search [4], and weather transformations motivate the optional fog task [5]. This work does not implement or benchmark those papers’ algorithms; all measured comparisons below are between this project’s own GA variants.

2. Shared search and dense perturbations

2.1 Fitness, genetic operators and protected feasibility

The fitness is the untargeted log-probability margin below, with probabilities floored at 10<super>−35</super> for numerical stability. A negative margin means a rival outranks the reference. Archive acceptance still checks the actual argmax and authoritative distance, so fitness ties or later infeasible candidates cannot erase a valid result.

m(x′) = log p<sub>y</sub>(x′) − max<sub>k ≠ y</sub> log p<sub>k</sub>(x′)

Dense search uses four truncation-selected elites. The best parent is chosen with probability 0.8; otherwise a parent is drawn from the elite pool. Spatial crossover occurs periodically and mutation introduces new structure. Infeasible elites may guide search at a harder radius, while a separate archive retains the lowest-distance classifier-verified image. Scalar bisection and explicit deletion are deterministic refinements around the GA, not genetic crossover or gradient estimates.

2.2 L∞: signed blocks and radius contraction

The chromosome is a full perturbation tensor bounded by its maximum component. Initial vertical sign stripes provide inexpensive diversity. A mutation replaces a randomly positioned square with independently signed channel values; periodic crossover copies another elite’s block. The initial radius is 0.4. After success, the target contracts by 0.8; repeated failure before discovery can expand it by 1.6. The spatial fraction decreases within a stage, permitting finer changes. Up to 12 final scalar-bisection evaluations reduce the successful direction without losing feasibility.

2.3 L2: structured energy and delayed boundary evolution

Early additive color-block mutations were unreliable. The selected representation initializes smooth positive/negative lobes over spatial tiles. Mutation recovers energy from two windows, handles their overlap once, and reallocates energy into a perturbed direction while projecting to the allowed Euclidean radius. The search starts at radius 30, with 0.8 contractions after success. The reported score is that radius’s squared-distance counterpart.

The final policy keeps global exploration for at least <b>512 main proposals and three successful stages</b>. It then evolves feasible directions near the boundary: target radius 0.99√D2, a 0.25 blend of parent and mutated direction, and up to three extra 0.98 scalar contractions after an accepted direction. Every probe is a charged model query; a failed probe never becomes the next feasible parent. Stagnation reduces the angular blend and can relax an overly hard boundary target.

3. Sparse changes and the fog genotype

3.1 L0: discovery, hierarchy, restarts and support crossover

Sparse discovery evolves large-valued channel-element changes. Once an adversary exists, discovery is bounded using a 0.55 fraction and a 900-call reference allocation, retaining the full fallback if no success has been found. This avoids spending most of the budget on a marginally smaller but difficult-to-prune discovery parent.

Refinement first tests whole-channel and spatial-group deletion. A failed group is split along its widest coordinate; accepted deletions preserve misclassification. Singleton deletions are revisited after other elements disappear, because classifier interactions can make an earlier failed deletion possible later. “Deletion minimal” therefore refers to tested removals at the current endpoint values, not a proof of the globally smallest support.

Protected restarts create independent parents. A further GA searches fixed-cardinality subsets of their combined loci: the target is one element smaller than the stronger parent. Four elites retain low-margin subsets, locus-exchange mutations and crossover/repair preserve target cardinality, and accepted children lower the target again. Overlapping loci inherit the stronger parent’s endpoint rather than adding amplitudes. The exact globally best feasible tensor survives failed restarts or offspring.

3.2 Fog: mixtures followed by integer-octave evolution

A genotype g is a list of positive-integer octaves and positive finite magnitudes, with at most 50 pairs. It produces the instructor’s deterministic Perlin fields, with generator seed 1. Opacity is clipped to [0,1] before blending toward the supplied normalized white color. The grading cost is only the sum of magnitudes; it is not an Lp image distance.

F<sub>g</sub> = Σ<sub>j</sub> a<sub>j</sub>P<sub>oⱼ</sub>, &nbsp; α = clip(F<sub>g</sub>, 0, 1), &nbsp; x′ = (1 − α)x + αw, &nbsp; D<sub>fog</sub>(g) = Σ<sub>j</sub> a<sub>j</sub>

The first GA uses 16 candidates over octave layers {1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24,32,48,64,96}. Pure layers and random mixtures seed the population; successful directions receive scalar boundary searches. Four parents are selected, crossover mixes their weights, and Gaussian mutations alternate broad and fine scales. The mixture phase has an 80-generation allowance and a stagnation stop. The remaining time evolves integer octaves 1–128 for up to 24 additional generations, using arithmetic crossover, local/global mutation and scalar magnitude reduction.

Layers are cached only within a search and model evaluations use batches of at most four. Fast search arithmetic is never substituted for the grading renderer: the final genotype is regenerated and classified exactly, with a protected verified mixture fallback. The common fog target is 270 s, below the 300 s limit; the final confirmation also timed mandatory exact rendering and classification. This is the assignment’s fog-like perturbation, not a calibrated atmospheric visibility model.

4. Development decisions and rejected approaches

Development followed a verified-baseline → hypothesis → controlled comparison → retention/rejection cycle. Equal query ceilings are distinguished from equal actual calls; equal time ceilings are distinguished from equal runtimes. The original starter’s failure sentinel 999999999 is not a measured perturbation score, and clean classification accuracy is not attack success.

The first 900-call L0 tank result of 2240 and the final saved result of 84 use different methods and budgets. They demonstrate the evolution of the saved artifact, not a controlled efficiency ratio. Similarly, some final fog artifacts originated in 90-second runs, while the final function permits 270 seconds. Successful phenotype protection was retained throughout.

Engineering checks that changed decisions

A noncontiguous input initially made flattened indexed writes modify a copy instead of the candidate; the implementation now makes the input contiguous. Export tests also caught a one-ulp endpoint change when reconstructing x + (image − x); the archive now preserves the exact evaluated tensor. These correctness checks matter near a classification boundary. Shape, dtype, finite values, thread restoration, randomness, archive protection and exact serialization are tested separately from real-model performance.

Later experiments showed that evidence from the five supplied images was insufficient for policy selection. The next page documents why the selected delayed-boundary method was retained despite mixed labelled development results, and why two scheduling alternatives were rejected.

9. Conclusions, limits and reproducibility

9.1 Conclusions supported by the measurements

Different metrics favored different representations: signed blocks control the largest component; structured energy plus feasible-direction evolution distributes L2 changes; explicit support deletion and parental-locus evolution reduce L0; octave/magnitude genes expose a compact fog family. The largest supplied-image sparse improvements came from removing redundant successful support. The fog extension benefited from a wider octave range and time the earlier stagnation stop left unused.

The failed compact rectangle, caching and reversible-window experiments are part of the result. Representation simplicity, fewer repeated calls, or an operator that sometimes improves the archive did not guarantee a better final distance. The clean baseline, saved-attack portfolio, fixed-call comparisons and actual-time confirmation answer different questions and were kept separate.

9.2 Limits and next investigations

Forty successful attacks cover only ten correlated inputs, one photo per class and one seed. Imagenette is ImageNet-derived and may overlap classifier pretraining. The 40 cases are an absolute assessment of one source, not a paired comparator or an estimate over all ImageNet classes. French horn remained costly: squared L2 4039.34 and L0 177; no global-minimality claim is justified. The confirmation bank is now consumed and cannot be treated as fresh independent validation after tuning.

Further development should test a distinct L2 spatial-coefficient representation and L0 support-space exhaustion/neighbor expansion, with matched actual-time controls and another seed. Such changes belong on development data and require a new independent bank before another generalization claim. The instructor’s true hidden evaluation remains unknown.

9.3 Tested execution environment and replay

Measured environment: Python 3.14.4; torch 2.15.0+cpu; torchvision 0.29.2+cpu; NumPy 2.5.4; Pillow 13.0.0; matplotlib 3.11.2; perlin-noise 1.13. CPU: AMD Ryzen AI 7 350, Linux/WSL2. Default search seed is 454; final confirmation uses 457. There is no GPU or gradient-based optimization.
